Your first word was getting eaten. We ate it. Sorry.

You'd press the shortcut and start talking right away, like a normal person, and Dictate would still be getting ready. Stretching. Finding the microphone. Looking for its keys. By the time it was listening you were three words in, and your email started with "the budget by Friday."

Now the microphone opens the instant the shortcut goes down, and we keep everything from that moment. First word, safe. First syllable, safe. That little "so" everybody starts sentences with? Safe. Unfortunately.

Second thing. Every update used to make macOS treat Dictate like a total stranger. "Who the hell are you? Turn Accessibility back on." Now every release is signed with the same certificate, so your Mac recognizes us after an update, like a dog that finally stops barking at the mailman.

Third thing. Sometimes a hesitation at the start came back as one lonely letter, like "É." Just "É." Standing there in its little hat. We delete it now.

The short version:

- Dictation starts the moment the shortcut is pressed, so your first words aren't lost.
- Releases are signed with one stable certificate, so Accessibility stays on after updates.
- A stray single letter like "É." at the start of an English dictation is removed.
