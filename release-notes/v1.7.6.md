Dictate reads your screen so it can spell names the way your screen does. Turns out it was reading your screen like a drunk reads a menu: it saw everything and remembered nothing useful.

Bug one. Your friend Brittni is right there on the screen. You say "Brittni." Dictate writes "Brittany." Here's why: Dictate *did* see her, then asked the Mac's spell checker, "Is Brittni a real word?" And the spell checker, which has memorized every baby name since 1990, said "yep, totally normal." So Dictate threw her in the bin. We built a name finder that throws out names. Outstanding work, everyone.

Bug two. A company called Arx Media is all over your screen, and Dictate writes "Arcs Media" every single time. Why? The Mac ships with a word list from a 1934 dictionary, and that dusty bastard says "arx" is an old word for a fortress. So Dictate went, "Ah, a common English word, nobody needs help with that," and ignored it. Thanks, 1934.

Bug three. PocketFM on screen, "Pocket FM" in your text. Two words where there should be one, like a sandwich somebody sat on.

All fixed. Dictate now keeps first names, company names and weird spellings as long as they aren't plain dictionary words. It also notices names that come in pieces, like "Arx Media," and sends them as one.

And because sending a hundred names to the speech engine makes it start hearing names you never said, it's pickier about that part now. Only names from the window you're typing in go out as hints, 20 at most. Everything else on both your screens is kept on your Mac and used to clean up afterwards. If you said something that sounds like a name on screen, it gets the screen's spelling: "Arcs Media" becomes Arx Media and "pocket FM" becomes PocketFM. But "arcs of light" stays arcs of light, because Media isn't next to it. We're idiots, but we're careful idiots.

And those 20 aren't just the first 20 it trips over anymore. Names near where you're typing win, the newest messages most of all, then names that keep showing up, then fancy ones like PocketFM. Your own name never takes a slot, because you don't dictate your own name, you weirdo.

Want to see what it sent? Click a dictation in History and it shows the names that went along with it. Now when it screws up, you can see exactly how.

That cleanup happens on your Mac, takes no AI, and adds no wait you could ever notice.

The short version:

- Fixed: first names the Mac's spell checker knows, such as Jessica, were dropped from screen reading. They're now kept.
- Fixed: names that happen to be rare dictionary words, such as Arx, were dropped. They're now kept.
- New: multi-word names on screen, such as "Arx Media", are recognized as one name.
- New: a name said in pieces, such as "pocket FM", is joined to match the screen, "PocketFM".
- New: a word that sounds like part of a name on screen is fixed when the rest of the name matches, so "Arcs Media" becomes "Arx Media".
- Changed: only names from the window you're typing in are sent as hints, at most 20. Names elsewhere on your displays are used only to fix the text afterwards.
- Changed: hints are ranked: names near where you're typing first, newest messages most, then names that appear often, then full or unusual names. Your own name is never sent.
- New: click a dictation in History to see the screen names sent with it.
- Fixed: a name that continues a sentence is no longer lowercased.
