Picture it. Your friend Brittni's name is right there on the screen. Dictate reads your screen. You say "Brittni." Dictate writes "Brittany." Like a bouncer who checked her ID, looked her in the eye, and still wrote down the wrong name.

Here's the stupid part. Dictate *did* see the name. Then it asked the Mac's spell checker, "Is Brittni a real word?" And the spell checker, which has apparently memorized every baby name since 1990, said "yep, totally normal word." So Dictate went, "Oh, a normal word, nobody needs help spelling that," and threw it in the bin. We built a name finder that tossed out the names. Outstanding work, everyone.

Now Dictate asks a better question: is this a word you'd find in a dictionary, or just a name the spell checker happens to know? Names get kept and sent along with your dictation, so they come back spelled the way your screen spells them. Names that are also plain words, like Mark or Grace, already come out right, so nothing changes there.

Bonus: when you keep talking in the middle of a sentence, Dictate stopped lowercasing names it thought were boring words. "brittni said yes" was a crime. It's over.

The first time Dictate meets a new word it takes a hair longer to check, about two thousandths of a second. It does that homework in the background while you switch apps, so your dictations don't wait on it.

The short version:

- Fixed: first names on screen that the Mac's spell checker accepts, such as Brittni or Jessica, were dropped, so they could come back misspelled. They're now kept.
- Fixed: a name at the start of a continued sentence no longer gets lowercased because the spell checker knew it.
- Dictate now checks words on screen in the background when you switch apps, so dictation doesn't wait on the spell checker.
