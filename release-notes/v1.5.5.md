You know that thing where you dictate a beautiful paragraph? Your best work. Honestly, Pulitzer stuff. And you let go of the key, and the text goes... nowhere.

Because you'd clicked on the desktop. You dictated into the desktop. The desktop does not want your paragraph. The desktop has never wanted anything.

So now there's **Paste Last Dictation.**

It's in the menu bar. It can also be a shortcut: go to **Settings → Shortcut → Paste last dictation** and pick a key combination, or a single right-side key like Right Option. We didn't pick one for you, because every key combination on a Mac already belongs to somebody, and we're not starting that fight.

It pastes your latest dictation exactly the way it was saved, wherever your cursor is. It still works after you quit and reopen Dictate. It won't do anything while you're mid-dictation, because pasting while you're still talking is how you get sentences inside sentences inside sentences.

From the menu, it waits about a tenth of a second before pasting, so the menu can finish leaving the room. Very polite. Like holding the door. For a menu.

And it costs dictation nothing. Not a millisecond. It only looks at your history when you ask, like a good friend, or a well-behaved cat.

The short version:

- New: **Paste Last Dictation** in the menu bar, and as an optional shortcut in Settings → Shortcut.
- It pastes your most recent dictation from History, exactly as saved.
- No default key. Set one yourself.
