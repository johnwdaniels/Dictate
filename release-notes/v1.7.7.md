You let go of the key. Inworld's servers sprint. The words come back. And then Dictate, the idiot, stops to do its paperwork *before* handing them over.

Here's what it was doing in that gap. First it made a backup of your clipboard, so it could put it back after pasting. If the last thing you copied was a big screenshot or a chunk of a spreadsheet, that backup could take a real moment. Then it asked the app you're typing in what's right before your cursor, so the spacing comes out right. Slack and Chrome take their sweet time answering that. Then it wrote the dictation into History. All of it while your words sat there waiting, like a cab idling outside while you look for your keys.

Now Dictate does that homework while Inworld is still working, which is time it was wasting anyway. When the words land, it pastes right away. History gets written after. The spell checker also stopped looking up every capitalized word you said, and now only checks the ones that look like a name on your screen.

How much faster? Usually a few milliseconds, sometimes a lot more if your clipboard is holding something fat. We also started writing down exactly how long each step takes, so next time we can tell you a real number instead of "trust us, bro."

Nothing about the microphone or the start sound changed. No new crackles. We checked our own crackles too, and those are a doctor's problem.

The short version:

- Faster: the clipboard backup and the text before your cursor are now read while Inworld finishes, not after.
- Faster: the dictation is pasted before it's saved to History.
- Faster: name fixing only runs the spell checker on words that look like a name on screen.
- New: Dictate logs how long each dictation takes from key release to paste, without the text, so speed can be measured.
- No changes to the microphone or the start sound.
